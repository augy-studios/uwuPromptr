Privacy

Last updated 15 November 2026

uwuPromptr is made by Augy Studios. This page says what uwuPromptr keeps about you, where it goes, and how to delete it. It covers the website at cue.uwuapps.org, the Android app (from Google Play or GitHub) and its Wear OS watch app, and the Windows, macOS and Linux apps.

The short version

  • No ads, and nothing about you is sold. The apps don't use your device's advertising ID and have no analytics or tracking.
  • Your scripts and settings stay on your device. There are no accounts, and nothing you write is sent to us.
  • The remote connects your devices straight to each other. Your script goes from one to the other, not through our server.
  • Uninstalling the app, or clearing the website's data, deletes everything it kept.

1. Your scripts and settings

Your scripts, the prompter's settings (font size, speed, mirroring and the rest), your theme and the remote's last ID are kept on your device only: in your browser's storage on the website, and in the app's own storage in the apps. uwuPromptr has no accounts and no server copy of anything you write.

The first time the 2.0 apps open after an update from version 1.0, they copy what the older app kept on the same device into their own storage. Nothing leaves the device.

2. The remote

The remote lets one device drive another. When you open Remote control, the prompter takes a six-character ID; the remote connects with it.

  • The two devices find each other through PeerJS's public introduction service, which sees both devices' IP addresses and the ID, but never your script.
  • They then talk to each other directly, encrypted, over WebRTC. The script, its settings and the remote's commands go only between your two devices. To find a route between them, each asks a STUN server (Google's or Cloudflare's) for its own public address.
  • Where the devices can't reach each other directly, uwuPromptr may relay the encrypted connection through its own TURN server, which passes the data on without being able to read it, and keeps no copy. The website hands out a short-lived password for it.
  • Connect nearby, without wifi (Android, with Google Play services) links two phones over Bluetooth and Wi-Fi Direct with Google's Nearby Connections, with no internet at all. The ID is checked inside the encrypted link and never broadcast.

3. The watch app

The Wear OS watch app talks only to uwuPromptr on your own phone, through Google's Wearable Data Layer. It sends play, pause and speed commands, and gets back the script's name, the clock and how far through it is. Nothing goes to us.

4. Report a bug

If you use Report a bug (in the side menu, or the remote's top bar), what you send goes to uwuPromptr's server and on to Augy Studios as a message from a Telegram bot. It contains:

  • what you typed, and a way to reach you, if you give one;
  • up to three pictures, if you add them: from your device, or a screenshot of a page of the app. A screenshot shows whatever is on that page, which can include your script;
  • what helps find the bug, all shown to you before it's sent: the app's version, which app (website, Android, desktop), your device and system as your browser or app describes them, and the page you were on (the prompter or the remote). Never a script's name or its words, unless a picture you add shows them.

uwuPromptr's server doesn't store reports. They're kept in Augy Studios' Telegram chats, which Telegram stores under its own privacy policy. To limit misuse, the server counts reports from each IP address, for a minute and, where set up, for an hour; then the count is gone. Each report gets an ID (like 20261115-7K3Q): quote it to have that report deleted.

5. Updates and notifications

  • The Android app from GitHub asks uwuPromptr's server whether there's a new version, and downloads it from GitHub, which sees your IP address.
  • New version alerts (Android, off unless you turn it on) use Firebase Cloud Messaging, Google's push service. Your phone joins a topic for uwuPromptr's new versions, and Google handles the address it sends notifications to. uwuPromptr's server keeps no list of phones.
  • The Android app from Google Play is updated by Google Play, and has no updater of its own.
  • The Windows, macOS and Linux apps check GitHub for new versions themselves, about once an hour.

6. The website

  • Google Analytics counts visits to the website's pages: which pages are opened, roughly where from, and on what kind of device and browser. It uses cookies to tell visits apart. It's on the website only, not in the apps. Your browser's settings or an ad blocker can stop it, and uwuPromptr works the same without it.
  • Google Fonts serves the website's font, so Google sees your IP address when it loads. The apps carry the font themselves.
  • For the remote, the website loads the PeerJS library from cdnjs (Cloudflare), only when you open it.

7. Android permissions

  • Internet: for the remote, Report a bug and the update check.
  • Notifications: only asked for when you turn on New version alerts.
  • Nearby devices (Bluetooth and Wi-Fi; location on Android 11 and older, which Android needs for Bluetooth there): only asked for when you use the offline remote. It isn't used to find where you are.
  • Install apps: the GitHub version only, to install its own updates when you choose Update. The Google Play version doesn't have it.
  • Keep the screen on: while the script scrolls or a remote is connected.

uwuPromptr doesn't use your contacts, camera, microphone, files or advertising ID, and doesn't record where you are.

9. Keeping and deleting your data

Everything uwuPromptr keeps is on your device, so you're in charge of it:

  • Delete a script from the Scripts menu, with its bin button.
  • Uninstall the app to delete everything it kept. On the website, clear the site's data in your browser's settings.
  • New ID in Remote control gives up the remote's ID straight away.
  • Bug reports can be deleted on request: email us and quote the report's ID.

10. Your choices and rights

You can leave out the contact details in a bug report, and keep New version alerts off. Depending on where you live (for example under the UK or EU GDPR, or Singapore's PDPA), you may have the right to see the data we hold about you, have it corrected or deleted, take a copy, or object to how it's used. Apart from bug reports, we hold none. Email us to ask, and we'll answer within 30 days. You can also complain to your local data protection authority.

11. Keeping it safe

Everything between the apps and uwuPromptr's server is sent over HTTPS, and the remote is encrypted from device to device. The keys to the bug report bot and the TURN server stay on the server. No service is perfectly secure, but if something goes wrong that affects your data, we'll tell you.

12. Children

uwuPromptr isn't made for children under 13, and we don't knowingly keep their data. If you think a child under 13 has sent us a bug report, email us and we'll delete it.

13. Changes to this page

If what uwuPromptr does with your data changes, this page changes with it, and the date at the top says when. Bigger changes are also said in the apps' release notes.
